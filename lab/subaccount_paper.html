Sub-Account Strategy Lab

A single-exchange spread-harvesting simulator using four independent sub-accounts (A, B, C, D) and two alternating spreads.

Each spread consists of two sub-accounts holding opposite positions in the same two assets:

  • Spread 1 = Account A + Account B
  • Spread 2 = Account C + Account D

Example (BTC/HYPE): Any pair of Hyperliquid perpetuals can be selected from the asset picker. The direction is fixed by design — Account A is always "long Coin A / short Coin B", Account B is always the opposite. Swap which coin is Coin A vs Coin B if you want the opposite direction.

For a BTC/HYPE pair (example):

  • Account A: Long HYPE / Short BTC
  • Account B: Short HYPE / Long BTC
  • Account C: Long HYPE / Short BTC
  • Account D: Short HYPE / Long BTC

Each spread behaves independently. When one account in an active spread reaches the triggering condition, that account closes immediately and the other account becomes the remaining account. The remaining account continues independently toward recovery, stop, liquidation, max hold, or end-of-data.

The triggering close activates the opposite spread. It does not wait for the remaining account to close. This means Spread 1 and Spread 2 can be active simultaneously, with up to four sub-accounts holding open positions at the same time.

The strategy has two separate concepts: entry logic (determines when a spread should open) and exit logic (determines which account triggers first and how the remaining account is managed). The exit logic is shared by all entry strategies — only the entry condition changes.

Spread Architecture

Spread 1 — Accounts A/B

When Spread 1 opens, Account A (long Coin A / short Coin B) and Account B (short Coin A / long Coin B) open simultaneously. As their P&Ls diverge, one account eventually reaches the triggering condition.

For example: A = −$1.20, B = +$2.50 → B reaches the trigger → B is the triggering account → B closes → A becomes the remaining account.

The triggering account is closed and its result is banked. The remaining account stays open until it: reaches the recovery target, hits the recovery stop, reaches max hold, is liquidated, or reaches the end of available data.

Important: "triggering account" and "remaining account" describe the exit sequence, not whether the account made or lost money. A remaining account can still close profitably.

Spread 2 — Accounts C/D

Spread 2 is an identical independent copy using C and D. Spread 2 uses the same entry and exit configuration unless separately configured.

Spread Rotation

This is the single most important behavioral rule in the entire application.

Spread 1 opens → A + B active
         ↓  A or B triggers
Triggering account closes
         ↓
Spread 2 opens → C + D active
         ↓  C or D triggers
Triggering account closes
         ↓
Spread 1 opens again → ...

Important: The remaining account from the previous spread is not required to close before the next spread opens.

12:30  Spread 1 opens       A active, B active
17:05  B triggers, closes    A remains open
17:10  Spread 2 opens        C active, D active    ← A is STILL open
06:55  D triggers, closes    C remains open
20:15  Spread 1 opens again  A + B active          ← C may still be open

Therefore the four sub-accounts can have overlapping states such as:

  • A = remaining, B = closed, C = remaining, D = closed
  • A = active, B = active, C = remaining, D = closed

This overlap is an important part of the strategy and is represented in the simulation, event log, AI analysis, and timeline UI.

Data Source

Historical Hyperliquid perpetual data is loaded from the local data-fetching layer. The simulator determines the usable simulation window from the overlap between the two selected assets.

  • 5-minute OHLC candles from Hyperliquid, fetched via the Hyperliquid SDK (candleSnapshot API) and stored locally.
  • The asset selector shows each coin's available history (e.g. "BTC ✓ 385.5d, 0.0h old"). Any Hyperliquid perpetual can be picked — the pair is not hardcoded.
  • Usable pair history = the common overlapping window. Example: if BTC has 385.5d and HYPE has 385.5d, the usable window is 385.5d. If they differ (e.g. BTC 385.5d, HYPE 210d), the shorter one applies (210d).
  • No hard-coded 17-day limit — the simulation period is bounded by available data, not an application-level restriction.
  • Bid/ask estimated from candle range (30% of high−low, minimum 0.01%).
  • Funding from the fundingHistory API, calculated independently per sub-account using actual funding rates at hourly settlement boundaries.
  • Because the two accounts within a spread hold opposite positions, funding should approximately offset while both are open. Once one account triggers and closes, the remaining account continues accumulating funding, creating residual funding P&L.
  • Missing candles, gaps, or insufficient history are reported rather than silently substituted.

Data Alignment

The two selected assets must be aligned to the same simulation timestamps. The usable simulation window begins at the first timestamp for which both assets have valid candle data and ends at the last timestamp for which both assets have valid candle data. The simulator must explicitly detect and report missing candles, timestamp gaps, or insufficient overlap. It must never silently forward-fill or fabricate missing market data.

Terminology

  • Triggering Account: The account within an active spread that reaches the P&L-difference/take-profit condition first. Previously called "winner." Does not imply the account produced the largest final profit.
  • Remaining Account: The other account in the spread that stays open after the triggering account closes. Previously called "loser." May ultimately recover for a profit, close around breakeven, hit the recovery stop, be liquidated, or reach the time limit. The terminology describes trade state, not P&L outcome.
  • Spread: A pair of opposing sub-accounts. Spread 1 = A + B, Spread 2 = C + D.
  • Cycle: A complete spread activation sequence: spread opens → triggering account closes → remaining account eventually closes. A cycle and a spread rotation are separate concepts. A cycle is complete only when the remaining account closes. However, the next spread is activated when the triggering account closes. Therefore, a new spread can begin while the previous cycle is still in recovery.

Modes

Single Spread: Only A/B are used. A + B open → one account triggers → triggering account closes → remaining account recovers/stops/expires → cooldown → A + B can open again. C/D remain inactive.

Gated Two-Spread: Both spreads are enabled. Spread 1 opens first. When either A or B triggers, the triggering account closes and Spread 2 opens. When either C or D triggers, the triggering account closes and Spread 1 opens again. The remaining account from the previous spread does not block the next spread from opening. This creates deliberate overlap between spreads. Use Copy from Spread 1 to duplicate Spread 1's configuration into Spread 2.

Entry Strategies

The real trading bot (spread_strategy.py) enters on a timer without market awareness. This lab tests whether market-aware entry conditions improve the baseline. All four strategies use the same exit engine.

  • Timer (baseline): Enter whenever cooldown expires. No market awareness. Reproduces the real bot's current entry philosophy and provides the control group.
  • BB on log spread: Enter only when log(mid_A) − log(mid_B) moves outside ±k standard deviations of its rolling mean. Bets on mean reversion from extremes. BB Period = rolling lookback in seconds (default 300s). BB Mult = standard-deviation threshold (default 2.0σ). Example: bb:upper(z=2.15).
  • BB + Volatility Gate: Same as BB, but entry additionally requires sufficient recent spread volatility. Filters out dead markets where the spread is technically extreme but too stagnant to produce a useful P&L divergence. Min Vol = minimum std of log spread (default 0.0008).
  • Momentum (trend-following): The opposite hypothesis to mean reversion. Enter when the spread is moving sufficiently quickly in one direction. ROC Period = rate-of-change lookback (default 60s). Min Mom = minimum |roc| (default 0.001). Example: mom:down(roc=-0.0012). If Momentum consistently outperforms BB, that suggests the useful behavior may be trend continuation rather than mean reversion.

Each cycle's entry signal is shown in the cycle log. The summary cards show how many entry attempts were rejected by the strategy's filter vs how many were accepted.

Shared Exit Parameters

These parameters are identical across entry strategies.

  • Coin A / Coin B: The two assets traded by every sub-account, picked from the Hyperliquid perpetuals list. Account A/C = long Coin A / short Coin B, Account B/D = short Coin A / long Coin B. Example: for BTC/HYPE, A/C = Long HYPE / Short BTC, B/D = Short HYPE / Long BTC. Swap which coin is A vs B to reverse the direction.
  • Days: Requested simulation lookback. The maximum selectable period is determined dynamically from the overlapping historical data available for both selected assets. The simulator must not assume a fixed maximum such as 17 days.
  • Notional: USD notional per leg per sub-account. A newly activated spread opens 2 accounts × 2 legs = 4 legs, so spread exposure = 4 × notional. If both spreads overlap, total live exposure can reach 8 × notional across A/B/C/D.
  • Entry fee / Exit fee: taker or maker. Uses the exchange's actual fee tier. This is the real fee rate — it is NOT multiplied by TP Mult.
  • TP Mult (take-profit multiplier): 0 = matches the real bot exactly — the triggering account must clear its own round-trip fees before closing. If > 0, the triggering account must earn (1 + TP Mult) × its round-trip fees in gross P&L. This sets the profit threshold, NOT the fee rate.
  • Min diff: the P&L difference between the two accounts required to trigger the first close. Effective trigger: max(min_diff, trigger_profit_floor).
  • Recovery exit: close the remaining account when its net P&L ≥ this value (after exit fees + funding). Default $0.0 = breakeven.
  • Recovery stop: hard stop on the remaining account. If the remaining account's gross P&L drops to this value, close immediately. Default −$2.00. This is the primary tail-risk control.
  • Max hold: maximum lifetime of a cycle from spread entry to remaining-account close. 0 = unlimited — the remaining account can run for hours or days. Set to e.g. 30m or 1h to bound the exposure.
  • Cooldown: time between the relevant spread becoming eligible for another entry. Each spread observes its own cooldown independently. The triggering close immediately makes the opposite spread eligible to open (subject to its own cooldown and entry strategy). The cooldown does not wait for the remaining account to close.

State Machine

Each spread has its own independent state. The four accounts have their own position states. The gated system is NOT one global FLAT → HEDGED → RECOVERY state.

Per-Spread State:

  • IDLE → HEDGED → TRIGGERED → RECOVERY → COMPLETE
  • HEDGED: Both accounts in the spread are open (A = open, B = open).
  • TRIGGERED: One account has closed (A = remaining, B = closed, or vice versa).
  • RECOVERY: The remaining account is being managed toward recovery, stop, max hold, liquidation, or end-of-data.
  • COMPLETE: Both accounts are closed.

Overlap is expected: Another spread can already be active while this spread is in TRIGGERED/RECOVERY. For example:

Spread 1 = RECOVERY (A = remaining, B = closed)
Spread 2 = HEDGED   (C = open, D = open)        ← simultaneously

This is expected behavior, not an error. "Spread completed" and "next spread opened" are two different events. The next spread is triggered by the triggering-account close, not by the remaining-account close.

Key Risk: Asymmetric Payoff + Overlapping Spreads

The strategy has an asymmetric payoff profile. A triggering account may bank a small, repeatable profit while the remaining account can incur a substantially larger loss. Therefore, profitability depends primarily on the distribution of remaining-account outcomes — especially recovery, stop, liquidation, and time-limit rates — rather than triggering-account win rate alone.

With two-spread gating, there is an additional risk: capital can remain exposed in a recovery leg while the opposite spread opens. Performance must be evaluated using: recovery rate, stop rate, liquidation rate, average triggering P&L, average remaining-leg P&L, spread-level net P&L, overlapping exposure, maximum simultaneous open legs, drawdown, fees, funding, and time in market. Win rate alone is not sufficient.

Three-Level Result Inspection

1. Overview: Net P&L, ROC, win rate, profit factor, max drawdown, equity curve, P&L distribution, Spread 1 vs Spread 2 performance, Account A/B/C/D performance, fee and funding totals, recovery vs stop statistics, and Spread P&L reconciliation:

Account A + Account B → Gross P&L → − fees ± funding → Net Spread P&L
Account C + Account D → Gross P&L → − fees ± funding → Net Spread P&L

This prevents the AI or UI from presenting a spread-level P&L without explaining how the individual account results produced it.

2. Cycle Timeline: Human-readable narrative of every spread activation showing entry → triggering account close (reason, P&L) → remaining account close (reason, P&L) → cycle net. Clearly shows overlap between spreads. Click any cycle to jump to that point on the price chart.

3. Raw Cycle Log: Forensic table with timestamp, spread, account, entry/exit prices, bid/ask estimates, gross P&L, fees, funding, net P&L, trigger difference, exit reason, duration. Sortable by any field.

Event Timeline

The authoritative chronological view. Each event identifies: timestamp, spread, account, event type, P&L, triggering/remaining state, and trigger reason. Alongside it, an account-state strip shows colored bars for each account (A, B, C, D) representing when positions were open, closed, in recovery, stopped, or liquidated. This makes interactions such as "Spread 2 opened while Spread 1 still had Account A open" immediately visible.

AI Evaluation

  • Explain Results: Sends simulation results to an LLM for a plain-English explanation covering overall performance, spread comparison, account performance, profit/loss drivers, fees/funding, recovery vs stop behavior, drawdown, and recommendations. The AI must reconcile spread-level numbers with the underlying account numbers.
  • AI Evaluate: Sends summary statistics, spread statistics, account statistics, cycle results, and the complete chronological event sequence to the LLM. Returns structured JSON with verdict, spread analysis, account analysis, exit analysis, spread interaction (evidence-based from events), risk flags, and an adjusted config with recommended: true/false and a reason. The AI uses the event sequence as evidence rather than inferring interaction behavior from aggregated statistics. It can identify facts such as "Spread 2 opened while Spread 1 still had a remaining account open." The AI is conservative and will not recommend live deployment from a single short simulation.
  • Providers: Gemini (fast, 1M TPM), Groq (fast), or local Ollama (private, runs on your machine).

Chart Controls

  • Normalized Price: Both assets normalized to 100 at the start of the simulation period.
  • Spread / Distance: Relative performance difference between the two assets (percentage points).
  • Markers: Trade markers — spread entry, triggering-account close, remaining-account close, recovery, stop, liquidation.
  • Spread Shading: Colored bands show when each spread has active positions. Different colors distinguish Spread 1 and Spread 2 so overlap is immediately visible.
  • Zoom: Mouse wheel / pinch to zoom, drag a box to zoom into a specific range, Ctrl+drag to pan, Reset Zoom for full view, Expand button for fullscreen.

Liquidation Engine (Hyperliquid Cross Margin)

Each sub-account has its own collateral pool. At every simulation candle, the simulator calculates account equity (= cash_balance + unrealized PnL) and compares against maintenance margin (from live Hyperliquid meta margin tables). If equity ≤ maintenance margin, the account is liquidated — all positions force-closed and the cycle ends. This supersedes the normal recovery/stop logic. Three modes: interval (determines if liquidation is possible within price ranges — labelled as possible, not historical fact), worst_case (uses adverse candle extreme), close (candle close only, deterministic). Hyperliquid's actual mark price updates ~3s and is a composite of oracle/book/external data — 5m OHLC candles cannot reproduce that path exactly. End-of-window positions are excluded from completed-cycle performance statistics.
